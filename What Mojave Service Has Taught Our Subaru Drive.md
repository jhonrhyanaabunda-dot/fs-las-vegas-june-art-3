## The Mojave severe-duty pattern

Subaru publishes both a normal-duty and a severe-duty maintenance schedule in every owner manual. The severe-duty schedule applies to vehicles operating in conditions including extreme heat, dust, short trips with the AC at full load, idling, towing, or climbing. Most Las Vegas driving meets at least three of those conditions year-round. Our service writer defaults every Clark County visit to severe-duty intervals, not because we want to sell more service, but because the wear patterns we have inspected on Clark County trade-ins confirm the published severe-duty cadence is what these vehicles actually need.

Three specific wear patterns predict the severe-duty fit:

**Engine oil degrades faster than the published mileage interval suggests.** We have pulled oil samples on 5,000-mile-old Forester oil in July that looked closer to 8,000-mile condition. Synthetic holds viscosity better than conventional, but Mojave heat compresses the safe service window. Our service team rotates between the 6-month and 6,000-mile interval, whichever comes first, on Las Vegas Subarus. Buyers who stretch all the way to the published 7,500-mile interval typically come in with darker, thinner oil than buyers who pull at 6,000 miles.

**Battery service runs 12 months earlier than the manual predicts.** Sustained 110°F+ underhood temperatures kill battery plates earlier than the published interval suggests. We have replaced batteries on Outback and Forester trade-ins at the 24-month mark when their original-region equivalents typically run 36 months. Load-test every spring before the heat hits.

**Cooling-system additive depletion runs ahead of the published schedule.** Subaru Super Coolant has a long published service life, but the additive package depletes faster under sustained heat. We have replaced coolant on Outbacks at the 5-year mark and found additive packages that should have been caught at year 3. We recommend annual coolant condition tests for Clark County drivers regardless of mileage.

## The actual service schedule by mileage

The core Subaru service intervals, applied to severe-duty Clark County operation:

**6,000 miles or 6 months (whichever comes first).** Engine oil and filter. Tire rotation. Multi-point inspection. Tire pressure adjustment. Battery load test (May through September).

**12,000 miles or 12 months.** All 6,000-mile items plus cabin air filter inspection, engine air filter inspection, brake fluid inspection, wiper blade inspection.

**30,000 miles.** All 6,000-mile and 12,000-mile items plus engine air filter replacement, cabin air filter replacement, brake fluid inspection, CVT fluid inspection, differential and transfer case fluid inspection, coolant condition test.

**60,000 miles.** All prior items plus spark plug replacement on non-turbo trims, brake fluid replacement, full inspection of belts and timing components, drivetrain fluid replacement (CVT and differential per Subaru's spec).

**90,000 miles.** All prior items plus CVT fluid replacement, transfer case fluid replacement (where applicable), front and rear differential fluid replacement, coolant replacement (if not done earlier on condition test), accessory drive belt replacement.

**120,000 miles.** All prior items plus second spark plug replacement on non-turbo trims, full cooling system service, full chassis inspection. Most Subaru engines built since 2014 reach this milestone on the original engine with no major mechanical work, and our service drive has seen 2017 Foresters at this mileage with strong compression numbers.

## Per-model variations

The core schedule applies across the Subaru lineup, but powertrain specifics shift the answer. Confirm with your VIN at our service writer's desk.

**Outback 2026.** 2.5L non-turbo and 2.4L turbo XT. Turbo models follow shorter spark-plug and oil intervals; 60,000-mile spark plug service moves to 45,000-mile on the XT.

**Forester 2026.** 2.5L non-turbo across the gas lineup. Standard 6,000-mile interval applies cleanly. The 2026 Forester Hybrid (Premium / Sport / Limited / Touring) has additional hybrid battery and inverter checks at the 30,000-mile and 60,000-mile services.

**Crosstrek 2026.** 2.5L only on the current model year. Lighter platform; pad life tends to be longer than on heavier siblings.

**Impreza 2026.** 2.0L non-turbo Sport. Cabin air filter access is straightforward for DIY between visits.

**Ascent 2026.** 2.4L turbocharged. Three-row weight increases brake wear; the turbocharger tightens spark-plug, oil, and oil-filter discipline.

**WRX and BRZ 2026.** Performance vehicles, shorter intervals on oil and brake fluid, more frequent spark plug service. Owner manual is canonical for the specific interval; bring the VIN.

## Frequently asked

**How often should I change my Subaru's oil in Las Vegas?** Subaru's standard interval is 6,000 miles or 6 months, whichever comes first, with full synthetic. In Las Vegas heat, with mostly short city trips or summer-traffic idling, treat the 6-month side of that window as the harder limit rather than waiting on mileage. Verify the spec for your model year in your owner manual.

**Do I need to follow the severe-duty schedule in Vegas?** For heat-sensitive items (engine oil, coolant condition, battery testing, AC service, and tire pressure monitoring), yes. The Subaru owner manual defines severe service to include extreme heat, dust, and short-trip driving in hot climates; Las Vegas qualifies on all three for most of the calendar year.

**Will skipping a scheduled service void my Subaru warranty?** Subaru's warranty requires scheduled maintenance to be performed on time using appropriate parts and fluids, and documented. A single missed interval will not automatically void coverage, but a pattern of skipped maintenance, or a failure that traces to a skipped service, can be denied. Keep records. Findlay Subaru of Las Vegas maintains your full service history on file when work is done with us.

**Can I have my Subaru serviced at a non-Subaru shop and keep my warranty?** Federal law (the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act) protects your right to use independent shops without voiding the warranty, provided the work is done correctly with appropriate parts. Subaru-certified technicians using Genuine Subaru parts give you the strongest position in a warranty claim and the deepest familiarity with the boxer-engine, symmetrical-AWD platform.
